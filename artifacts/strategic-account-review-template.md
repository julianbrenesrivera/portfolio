## Purpose

This is a generic example of how I think about turning account performance into a structured customer conversation. It is not a PayPal, Amazon, AO2, or Workday internal template.

## 1. Executive summary

Keep the opening focused on decisions rather than a data dump.

- What changed since the last review?
- What is working?
- Where is the biggest opportunity or risk?
- What decision or alignment is needed in this meeting?

## 2. KPI snapshot

| Area | Current signal | Direction | Business question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth | Synthetic example | ↑ / → / ↓ | What is driving the movement? |
| Adoption | Synthetic example | ↑ / → / ↓ | Which use cases or segments are underpenetrated? |
| Commercial activity | Synthetic example | ↑ / → / ↓ | Where should the next growth conversation focus? |
| Operational health | Synthetic example | ↑ / → / ↓ | Is anything blocking execution? |

The metrics should change by business model. The point is to connect each metric to a business question.

## 3. Opportunity map

For each opportunity:

**Observation**  
What does the account data or customer context suggest?

**Business relevance**  
Why does it matter to the customer?

**Proposed action**  
What should happen next?

**Owner / stakeholder**  
Who needs to participate?

**Review point**  
When and how will progress be revisited?

## 4. Risks and dependencies

A strong account review should surface blockers explicitly.

- customer dependency;
- internal dependency;
- data gap;
- operational risk;
- timing constraint.

## Operating principle

The useful output of a business review is not the presentation itself. It is shared understanding of:

**where the account stands → what matters next → who owns the action → how progress will be reviewed**

That pattern connects my work across strategic accounts, ecommerce, and enterprise payments.
